# V8: the Arbeitnow rate-limit window

Status: **complete (2026-10-18). The window length is not observable: every response came from Cloudflare's
cache. The cap formula stays, for the reason under Decision.** The by-product finding on the feed order (below)
was ruled on by the controller (Ruling T10-a, under Decision).

## Assumption

Arbeitnow's `x-ratelimit-limit: 50` counts requests in a window whose length is unknown; the page cap of §5.3.4,
`min(10, max(3, ceil(interval_h / 30) + 1))`, keeps a 7-day catch-up (7 pages) within it (spec §5.3.4, §22 V8).
Acceptance: the sequence of `x-ratelimit-remaining` determines the window (a reset within 2 h gives its length;
none means ≥ 2 h); otherwise the cap's upper bound is lowered.

## Method

`scripts/spikes/record_sources.py arbeitnow --phase k` (free, 6 requests, the budget's maximum): phase 1 `page=1`
at 2026-10-18T20:53:22Z, phase 2 `page=2` at 21:13:54Z (20 min), phases 3–6 `page=1` with only the headers and
the meta kept, at 40, 60, 90 and 120 minutes. The recorder refuses a phase before its time and never re-runs one.
Phases 2–5 ran in a background job. A container restart killed it after phase 5, and a re-created job ran
phase 6 at its own time (120 min).

- **Every response came from Cloudflare's edge cache** (`cf-cache-status: HIT`; the origin's `cache-control:
  private, max-age=432000` is not what governs it). The cache of `page=1` was refilled every hour on the second
  (20:29:22, 21:29:22, 22:29:23), so its edge TTL is about 3600 s; `page=2` is a separate cache entry with its own
  fill time. The body changed only at a refill.
- **The rate-limit headers are frozen.** They belong to the origin response that filled the cache, so
  `x-ratelimit-remaining` was 49 in all six responses, across three different fills: each fill was the first
  request of its window, and none of our requests reached the origin. The window cannot be measured through the
  cache: no decrement, so no reset, was observable.
- **A page can be up to an hour behind the feed** (the largest `age` was 3274 s, 55 min).
- A page holds **300 items**; `links.next` points to the next page (`page=2`, then `page=3`), and `links.prev`
  and `links.last` are present with `null`.

### By-product: the feed is not ordered by `created_at` across pages

Each page is **four runs, each newest first** (items 0–99, 100–149, 150–199, 200–299; `created_at` rises at items
100, 150 and 200 on both pages). Page 1 spans 02:32 to 20:00 on 2026-10-18. Page 2, read from a cache filled
17 minutes after page 1's, starts its last run at **20:00:44 on 2026-10-18** and spans back to 14:40 on
2026-10-16. No slug is on both pages, and the page-2 items newer than page 1's oldest item existed when page 1's
cache was filled. So **no page bounds the creation times of the pages after it**: a traversal that stops once it
"has passed `newest_covered_upper − 2 h`" (§5.3.4) on page 1 would leave newer items unread on page 2 and later
pages. The first two runs continue across the page boundary (page 1's run ends at 11:55 and page 2's begins at
11:55, and the same for 14:40); the last run does not.

## Decision

- **Controller ruling T10-a** (applies T6-d; recorded as an M1 Deviation by the controller): Arbeitnow reads to
  the page cap on every run, a never-covered cell included, and only the end of the feed (an empty `data` or no
  `links.next`) stops it earlier; there is no time-based stop. Coverage records only what the reads prove. Task 10
  derived the bound from the recordings: runs 1–3 continue exactly across the page boundary, but page 2's fourth
  run opens at 20:00:44, 12 s below page 1's newest item, and reaches back 53 h. So a read from page 1 to the end
  covers `[oldest item read, newest item read)`, its upper bound clamped to the earliest fill of its pages (see
  Freshness, below: the part above it is an open `unknown_continuation` gap), and a read stopped by the cap (or by
  `x-ratelimit-remaining ≤ 5`) covers nothing. Such a read records the gap from the cell's accounting frontier
  to its newest item. The bound "everything newer than the newest of the runs' oldest items" fails on this data:
  after page 1 it is 19:30:49, and page 2 holds five items at 20:00:43–44. A seek leg proves nothing either, and
  gaps close only through a read to the end. The fixture transport serves page ≥ 3 as `page_end`, so a fixture
  run reads pages 1, 2 and the end and is covered (`sources/arbeitnow.py`, `tests/contract/test_arbeitnow.py`).
- **The page cap formula stays.** The acceptance's first branch ("the window length is determined") is not met,
  and cannot be met from outside the cache. The cap is still safe:
  - The origin counts only the requests that reach it, which are cache misses. A miss response carries the live
    `x-ratelimit-remaining`, so the `x-ratelimit-remaining ≤ 5` stop of §5.3.4 sees the real count exactly when
    it matters. A hit carries a frozen 49 and costs the origin nothing.
  - One run sends at most 10 requests (the cap's upper bound), at 1 request per 2 s, which is below 50 in any
    window of any length. A 7-day catch-up is 7 pages.
  - Between runs, the edge cache absorbs repeated reads of a page within its hour.
- **Freshness:** a page can be up to about 1 h stale. Under T10-a no overlap stop exists (no time stops a run,
  so §5.3.4's 2 h overlap `newest_covered_upper − 2 h` has nothing left to bound); staleness bounds the coverage
  instead: a read to the end covers nothing newer than the earliest fill instant of its pages (`date − age`,
  the "Cache filled" column above), and the newer part of what it read stays an open gap, labelled
  `unknown_continuation` (fix wave F4, ledger L137, Ruling FR-o; `sources/arbeitnow.py` `filled_at`,
  `covered_span`). A cached page (`cf-cache-status` HIT, the signal of every response here) whose `Age` is absent
  or unreadable proves nothing above the cell's previous covered bound; a joined `Age` takes the largest value
  (Ruling FR-q), and an `Age` reads only as RFC 9111 delta-seconds, ASCII digits up to 2^31 (no sign, fraction
  or exponent; any other part makes it unreadable; fix wave F6). An item created after a page was made can be
  missing from that page, and by the time a later page was made it may sit on an earlier one, so the read
  proves nothing past the first fill. An older item that moves from a page made later to a page made earlier
  can still be missed (Ruling T10-b, the accepted residual risk).
- **The feed order (ruled: T10-a, plan Deviation 27):** §5.3.4 states "newest `created_at` first", and its
  coverage intervals, gaps and seek-by-count assume a feed ordered by time. Across pages that does not hold,
  so the time-based stop is not a safe stop for Arbeitnow, and a "contiguous run of fully read items" on the
  `created_at` axis is not what one page gives. The ruling took the first option this spike offered: every run
  reads to the page cap (3 pages for any interval up to 60 h, below the limit by the argument above) and records
  coverage only from what the reads prove; no seek leg is planned. This spike does not change the spec. The
  committed `page1` and `page2` are trimmed to the first 30 items of the first run, so they do not show the four
  runs; Task 10 derived its order fixtures from them by editing `created_at`.
